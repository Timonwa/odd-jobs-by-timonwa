I kept building the same small thing: a page that does one annoying job so I could stop doing it by hand. Count the words. Fix the slug. Turn a finished draft into posts worth publishing.

They live in one place now, and it's called **Odd Jobs** — the small, repetitive work that comes attached to writing and code.

## What's live today

**Nine tools, all free, no account.** Two of them use AI: [Article to SEO Meta](https://odd-jobs.timonwa.com/article-to-seo-meta) writes titles and descriptions sized to what Google actually shows, and [Article to Social Posts](https://odd-jobs.timonwa.com/article-to-social-posts) turns a finished piece into posts shaped for each network. Both have a free daily allowance, and you can [add your own Gemini key](https://odd-jobs.timonwa.com/blog/get-a-gemini-api-key) to skip the limit.

The other seven — word and character counts, case conversion, slugs, reading time, hashes, lorem ipsum, SVG to JSX — run entirely in your browser. Nothing you paste leaves the page.

There's also a [blog](https://odd-jobs.timonwa.com/blog) with guides on working better, and a [shop](https://odd-jobs.timonwa.com/shop) of templates and starters. Most of the shop is free.

## What lands in your inbox

**One issue a month.** New tools, new guides, and the parts I got wrong along the way. That's the whole commitment — no drip sequence, no "just checking in", and one click to unsubscribe whenever it stops earning its place.

Worth being precise, because "newsletter" gets used for everything: the monthly issue is the newsletter. If something ships mid-month that genuinely can't wait, that's a separate short announcement, and it's rare. Receipts and account emails aren't part of this list at all.

## Why any of this exists

Every tool here started as a job I was doing by hand and got tired of. That's the filter — if it isn't annoying enough to automate, it doesn't get built. The guides exist for the same reason: the interesting part usually isn't the tool, it's the workflow that made the tool necessary.

The code is open source under AGPL-3.0, so if a tool misbehaves you can read exactly why, open an issue, or fix it yourself.

## Next issue

Whatever ships between now and then, plus what I learn building it. If there's a job you'd most like to stop doing by hand, that's how the next tool gets chosen.
