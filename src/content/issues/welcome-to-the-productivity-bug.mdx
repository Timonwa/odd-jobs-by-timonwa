I kept building the same kind of thing: a small page that does one annoying job so I could stop doing it by hand. Count the words. Fix the slug. Turn a finished draft into posts I can actually publish. Nine of those now live in one place, and this is the first note to the people using them.

## What's actually here

Nine tools, free, no account:

- **Two AI ones** — [Article to SEO Meta](https://tools.timonwa.com/article-to-seo-meta) sizes titles and descriptions to what Google actually displays, and [Article to Social Posts](https://tools.timonwa.com/article-to-social-posts) turns a finished piece into platform-shaped posts you copy and paste yourself.
- **Seven instant ones** — word and character counts, case conversion, slugs, reading time, hashes, lorem ipsum, and SVG to JSX. These run entirely in your browser. Nothing you paste leaves the page.

The AI tools have a free daily allowance, and if you'd rather not share it, you can [drop in your own Gemini key](https://tools.timonwa.com/blog/get-a-gemini-api-key) for unlimited runs — it stays in your browser, never on my server.

## Why the rename

This used to be "Tools by Timonwa", which described who made it and nothing else. **The Productivity Bug** describes what it's for: the itch to stop doing the same manual thing twice.

The practical difference is that the site is no longer only tools. There's a blog now, and this newsletter, because the interesting part usually isn't the tool — it's the workflow that made the tool necessary.

## What lands in your inbox

Not much, deliberately. **I'd rather send six good issues a year than fifty forgettable ones**, so this arrives when a tool ships or when I've actually learned something worth passing on. No drip sequence, no "just checking in", unsubscribe in one click whenever it stops being worth it.

When it does arrive, expect the same shape as this: what changed, what I got wrong, and what you can use today.

## Building in the open

The code is open source under AGPL-3.0. If a tool misbehaves you can read exactly why, open an issue, or fix it — several of the rough edges on the current tools came from people telling me what broke.

## One ask

**Reply to this email and tell me the piece of busywork you'd most like to stop doing by hand.** That's genuinely how the next tool gets chosen — the SEO meta tool exists because three people described the same problem in slightly different words.
